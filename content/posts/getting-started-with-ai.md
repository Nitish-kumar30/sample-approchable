If you've tried using AI tools and felt like you were missing something, you're not alone. The gap between "I typed something and it gave me a mediocre answer" and "I use AI to do real, high-leverage work every day" isn't about having better prompts — it's about having a better mental model.

## The One Thing That Changes How You Use AI

Large language models don't "think." They predict. Every word in a response is the model's best guess about what word should come next, given everything in the context window. Once you internalize that, three things click into place:

1. **Why specificity matters** — Vague input produces statistically average output. Specific, contextual input constrains the prediction space toward what you actually need.
2. **Why hallucinations happen** — The model has no mechanism for "I don't know." It produces the most plausible-sounding continuation of your prompt, which is sometimes wrong.
3. **Why iteration beats one-shot prompting** — You're not submitting a form. You're having a conversation with a very fast, very knowledgeable collaborator who needs guidance.

## Where to Start

Don't start with productivity hacks. Start with one real task you already do — writing, analysis, research, coding, whatever — and use AI on that task until you hit a wall. The wall is the learning. Figure out why the output wasn't what you wanted, and adjust.

That loop, repeated deliberately, is how people go from "AI is kind of useful" to "I can't imagine working without it."
