Here's the most common thing I see when people share their AI prompts: they're too short, too vague, and written like a Google search.

"Write me a marketing email."

"Summarize this."

"Help me with my presentation."

These prompts will get you mediocre output every single time — not because the AI is bad, but because you've given it almost nothing to work with.

## The Core Mistake: Treating AI Like a Search Engine

Search engines are designed to infer what you want from minimal input. You type "best coffee shops NYC" and it surfaces relevant results. That trained us to communicate in fragments.

AI models don't work that way. They work best when you give them **role, context, task, and format** — the same things you'd give a capable human colleague before asking them to do something important.

Compare these two prompts:

**Weak:** `Write me a marketing email for my course.`

**Strong:** `You're a conversion copywriter. Write a 200-word email to professionals who are curious about AI but haven't taken the leap yet. The email should feel warm and direct, not hype-y. It's promoting a 6-week cohort called Approachable that teaches Claude AI tools. End with a single clear CTA to book a call.`

The second prompt takes 20 extra seconds to write. The output difference is enormous.

## The Fix: Use the RCTF Framework

Before you send any prompt, ask yourself four questions:

1. **Role** — What expert should Claude be playing? ("You are a senior product manager…")
2. **Context** — What do I need it to know? ("This is for a B2B SaaS audience of 500 subscribers…")
3. **Task** — What exactly do I want? ("Write a subject line + 3 bullet points…")
4. **Format** — How should the output look? ("Use plain text, no headers, keep it under 150 words.")

You don't need all four every time. But the more you provide, the better the output — and the less back-and-forth you'll need.

## One More Thing: Treat It as a Conversation

The best prompt isn't always the first one. Send something decent, review the output, then refine it in the same thread:

> "Good — now make the tone more casual and cut the last paragraph."

> "Rewrite this, but lead with the outcome instead of the problem."

Iteration is free. Use it.

The people who get the most out of AI aren't writing magic prompts. They're just treating it like a talented colleague who needs proper direction — not a vending machine you shake until something falls out.
